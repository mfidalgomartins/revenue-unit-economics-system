**One question drives the whole system: is growth sustainable, or just expensive?**

Top-line revenue growth can hide weak acquisition efficiency, fragile retention, or margin erosion. The pipeline calculates LTV/CAC by channel, cohort retention, contribution margin, and bounded spend-reallocation scenarios, then publishes the results as an interactive dashboard, chart pack, and analytical PDF.

**→ [Open the live dashboard](https://mfidalgomartins.github.io/revenue-unit-economics-system/)**

## What it diagnoses

| Question | Method |
|----------|--------|
| Which acquisition channels deserve budget? | LTV/CAC ratio and payback period per channel, color-coded against the 3:1 scale threshold |
| Is margin holding as revenue scales? | Monthly contribution margin trend with margin-rate tracking |
| Which cohorts decay fastest? | Revenue and activity retention curves from signup through month 24 |
| Where is margin being diluted? | Contribution margin breakdown by segment, region, and product |
| What does the reallocation envelope look like? | Bounded scenario engine: best / base / worst case under CAC and LTV elasticities |

## Data scope

The fixed baseline seed generates 9,000 customers, 69,950 transactions, and daily marketing spend for six acquisition channels from January 1, 2023 through December 31, 2025. Customers span four segments and four regions. All source data is synthetic and regenerated by the pipeline.

## Pipeline

```
data generation → validation → profiling → feature engineering →
core analysis → scenario engine → curated charts + dashboard →
supporting documentation + PDF report → QA gate
```

Each stage writes reproducible outputs under `outputs/` and `data/processed/`. A central metric registry (`src/governance/metric_registry.py`) defines LTV, CAC, payback, and efficiency thresholds in one place; analysis, dashboard, and validation all read from it.

## Metric contracts

| Metric | Definition |
|--------|------------|
| Contribution margin | Revenue minus direct delivery cost |
| Observed LTV | Mean cumulative contribution margin per acquired customer, including customers with zero transactions |
| CAC | Full-window channel spend divided by customers acquired through that channel |
| Approximate payback | CAC divided by average monthly contribution margin per acquired customer |
| Cohort activity retention | Active customers in month *n* divided by active customers in cohort month 0; inactive mature months are retained as zeros |
| Scenario uplift | Simulated contribution change under explicit CAC/LTV response assumptions; not a forecast |

## Methodology

- Acquisition economics use full-window channel spend and all acquired customers, including customers with no transactions.
- Cohort retention uses monthly signup cohorts and preserves mature inactive months as zero activity.
- Scenario analysis reallocates spend using explicit CAC and LTV elasticities, channel-efficiency thresholds, and scale-up caps.
- Five deterministic seeds test whether the scenario direction is stable under the same synthetic data-generating process.

## Limitations

- Data is synthetic. The pipeline demonstrates analytical method, not a real market.
- LTV is observed contribution margin per customer over the available window, not a forward forecast.
- CAC is period-level spend divided by customers acquired in the channel; no multi-touch attribution.
- Cohort retention is indexed to customers active in signup month, not every signup.
- Scenario outputs apply bounded CAC/LTV elasticities and cap channel-level scale-up at 100%; excess budget is held back when capacity is exhausted.
- Seed sensitivity repeats the same synthetic data-generating process; it tests stability, not external validity.
- Revenue and cost are intentionally right-skewed. A small share of transactions has negative contribution margin to preserve realistic cost-to-serve exceptions.
